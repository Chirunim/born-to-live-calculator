Methodology & Sources

This page explains every step of the calculation and links to the data behind each figure. All source figures live in data/figures.json in the source repository.

Step 1 — Estimating your tax (T)

We calculate two components of your UK tax:

  • Income Tax — applied to your taxable income (gross salary minus personal allowance) at the rates for your region (rest-of-UK or Scotland).
  • Employee National Insurance (Class 1) — applied to earnings above the Primary Threshold at 8%, and above the Upper Earnings Limit at 2%.
T = Income Tax + National Insurance

Figures used:

  • Personal Allowance: £12,570 (tapers to £0 above £100,000) — gov.uk/income-tax-rates, 2026–27
  • rUK Income Tax bands: 20% / 40% / 45% — same source
  • Scottish Income Tax bands: 19% / 20% / 21% / 42% / 45% / 48% — gov.uk/scottish-income-tax, 2026–27
  • NI Primary Threshold: £12,570 · Upper Earnings Limit: £50,270 · rates: 8% / 2% — gov.uk/national-insurance-rates-letters, 2026–27

This is an annual approximation. NI is technically calculated per pay period; using annual salary slightly overstates NI for monthly-paid workers near the thresholds. The difference is small and does not affect the aid result materially.

Step 2 — Identifying UK mother and child health aid

Rather than estimating a chain of ratios, we go directly to FCDO's raw disbursement data and sum the rows that correspond to health aid reaching mothers and children.

We use FCDO's Statistics on International Development: final UK ODA spend 2024 — specifically the data underlying SID 2017–2024 (ODS, 19.7 MB) — filtering to calendar years 2022–2024 and averaging across the three years. Rows are selected by the sector code FCDO records against each disbursement.

LOW definition — core mother and child health programmes:

  • 13020 — Reproductive health care
  • 12240 — Basic nutrition
  • 12250 — Infectious disease control
  • 12262 — Malaria control
  • Gavi (Vaccine Alliance) multilateral core contributions — identified by channel of delivery, not sector code (see note below)

HIGH definition — adds broader child-relevant programmes:

  • 12220 — Basic health care
  • 13030 — Family planning
  • 12261 — Health education

Gavi note: Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, procures and delivers vaccines for children in the world's poorest countries. UK core contributions to Gavi are pooled across Gavi's entire programme and recorded in FCDO data against Gavi as the channel of delivery rather than against a single sector code. We include them in both LOW and HIGH because vaccines are a direct child health intervention. Earmarked Gavi projects (which carry specific sector codes) are captured automatically by the code filter above.

childHealthODA = 3-year average annual UK disbursements to above codes (2022–2024)
  • LOW estimate: £486m/year (3-year average)
  • HIGH estimate: £661m/year (3-year average)

The computation is reproducible via scripts/compute_child_health.py in the source repository.

Step 3 — Your contribution to mother and child health aid (C)

HMRC's Annual Tax Summary allocates every taxpayer's bill across government spending areas in proportion to HM Treasury's total expenditure on services (£1,156.4bn in 2024–25). We use the same denominator to convert your tax into a proportional share of the UK's health aid reaching mothers and children.

C = T × (childHealthODA ÷ expenditure on services)

C is the estimated amount of your tax that becomes health aid reaching mothers and children through UK official development assistance in a given year.

We also show a per-day figure: C ÷ 365.

  • Expenditure on services: £1,156.4bn (£1,156,393m) — HM Treasury — Public Expenditure Statistical Analyses 2025, 2024–25
  • Method — HMRC — How public spending was calculated in your tax summary, 2024–25

Step 4 — What C buys (unit costs)

We convert C into three tangible items, using one cost for each. All three costs are UNICEF Supply Division purchase prices: what UNICEF pays suppliers for each item. They exclude delivery (shipping, storage, distribution and health workers' time), so the real cost of getting each item to a child is higher, and the item counts shown are best read as an upper bound. Prices are UNICEF published purchase prices: an awarded vaccine price (2026), a weighted average bednet price (2021), and indicative catalogue prices for ORS and zinc.

All costs are in USD and converted to GBP at the Bank of England 2024 annual average rate (1 USD = £0.7874, source: Bank of England).

  • Fully vaccinates one child (3 doses) — pentavalent vaccine (DTP-HepB-Hib): $0.85 per dose × 3 doses = $2.55. $0.85 is the 2026 awarded price for the 10-dose vial, the middle of the four 2026 awards ($0.78–$0.90) — UNICEF Supply Division — DTP-HepB-Hib (pentavalent) vaccine price data, updated 15 January 2026
  • Bednet (long-lasting insecticidal net): $1.94 per net, UNICEF's weighted average price in 2021, the latest available UNICEF figure — UNICEF Supply Division — LLIN market and supply update, October 2022, page 7
  • Full ORS + zinc treatment: $0.225 per course. The course is UNICEF's co-pack S1580022 (2 ORS 1 L sachets + 10 zinc 20 mg tablets). The co-pack has no listed price, so we add up its parts: 2 ORS sachets (S1561120, $7.90 per box of 100) = $0.158, plus 10 zinc tablets (S1580020, $0.67 per pack of 100) = $0.067 — UNICEF Supply Catalogue indicative prices, accessed 22 November 2026

Step 5 — Two estimates

There is no single agreed answer to which UK aid counts as health aid reaching mothers and children, so we show two estimates side by side. They use the same tax, the same method and the same unit costs, and differ only in which kinds of health aid they count (Step 2):

  • Core mother and child programmes (the LOW definition, £486m a year): reproductive health care, basic nutrition, infectious disease and malaria control, plus UK core funding for Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance.
  • Including wider basic health (the HIGH definition, £661m a year): everything in the core estimate, plus basic health care, family planning and health education.

Neither is a best guess with a margin of error around it. Each is a calculation from published figures, and the difference between them shows how much the answer depends on that choice.

Before the cuts (2022–24) vs Now (2026–27 estimate)

This section compares UK aid before the cuts with now. It shows the UK-wide figure first, then your own share.

UK mother and child health aid, per year. "Before" is the 2022–24 average from Step 2 (£486m–£661m), from the core estimate to the estimate including wider basic health (Step 5). We show it in pounds only. We do not convert the UK-wide budget into items: dividing all of it by purchase prices would overstate what it achieves, because aid also pays for delivery, health workers and much else. Item equivalents appear only for your own share, labelled "roughly equivalent to". "Now" (2026–27) is estimate pending ONE data team: there is no published 2026–27 figure on the same definition yet, so we show a placeholder rather than a guess.

UK pledges, per year. Gavi and Global Fund pledges cover several years, so we divide each total by the number of years it covers. This assumes the money is spread evenly.

  • Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance — before: £1.65bn for 2021–2025 = £330m a year (GOV.UK, 3 June 2020); now: £1.25bn for 2026–2030 = £250m a year (GOV.UK, 25 June 2025). Change: −24%.
  • The Global Fund — before: £1bn for 2023–2025 (seventh replenishment) = £333m a year (GOV.UK, 14 November 2022); now: £850m for 2026–2028 (eighth replenishment) = £283m a year (GOV.UK Biological Security Strategy implementation report, 14 July 2026; also in the Foreign Secretary's statement of 19 March 2026). Change: −15%.

The pledges table is UK-wide only. Gavi core contributions are already counted inside mother and child health aid (Step 2). The Global Fund covers HIV, TB and malaria for all ages. Do not add the pledges to the mother and child health aid figure.

Your share. Your share covers mother and child health aid only, using the same method as Step 3: your tax × UK mother and child health aid ÷ total expenditure on services. Both columns use the latest published total (2024–25), so the change you see comes only from the change in UK aid.

What this estimate does not include

  • Multilateral aid via UNICEF, World Bank, and other multilaterals (beyond Gavi) — these are harder to attribute to specific health sub-sectors and are not included.
  • Non-monetary UK contributions (technical assistance, policy influence).
  • Tax paid outside Income Tax and NI (e.g. VAT, capital gains) — methodologically complex and beyond the HMRC Annual Tax Summary methodology we follow.
