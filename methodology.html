Methodology & Sources

This page explains every step of the calculation and links to the data behind each figure. All source figures live in data/figures.json in the source repository.

Step 1 — Estimating your tax (T)

We calculate two components of your UK tax:

  • Income Tax — applied to your taxable income (gross salary minus personal allowance) at the rates for your region (rest-of-UK or Scotland).
  • Employee National Insurance (Class 1) — applied to earnings above the Primary Threshold at 8%, and above the Upper Earnings Limit at 2%.
T = Income Tax + National Insurance

Figures used:

  • Personal Allowance: £12,570 (tapers to £0 above £100,000) — gov.uk/income-tax-rates, 2024–25
  • rUK Income Tax bands: 20% / 40% / 45% — same source
  • Scottish Income Tax bands: 19% / 20% / 21% / 42% / 45% / 48% — gov.uk/scottish-income-tax, 2024–25
  • NI Primary Threshold: £12,570 · Upper Earnings Limit: £50,270 · rates: 8% / 2% — gov.uk/national-insurance-rates-letters, 2024–25

This is an annual approximation. NI is technically calculated per pay period; using annual salary slightly overstates NI for monthly-paid workers near the thresholds. The difference is small and does not affect the aid result materially.

Step 2 — Identifying UK child health ODA

Rather than estimating a chain of ratios, we go directly to FCDO's raw disbursement data and sum the rows that correspond to child health.

We use the FCDO Statistics on International Development — underlying data 2017–2024, filtering to calendar years 2022–2024 and averaging across the three years.

LOW definition — core child health programmes:

  • 13020 — Reproductive health care
  • 12240 — Basic nutrition
  • 12250 — Infectious disease control
  • 12262 — Malaria control
  • Gavi (Vaccine Alliance) multilateral core contributions — identified by channel of delivery, not purpose code (see note below)

HIGH definition — adds broader child-relevant programmes:

  • 12220 — Basic health care
  • 13030 — Family planning
  • 12261 — Health education

Gavi note: Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, procures and delivers vaccines for children in the world's poorest countries. UK core contributions to Gavi are pooled across Gavi's entire programme and recorded in FCDO data against Gavi as the channel of delivery rather than against a single CRS purpose code. We include them in both LOW and HIGH because vaccines are a direct child health intervention. Earmarked Gavi projects (which carry specific purpose codes) are captured automatically by the code filter above.

childHealthODA = 3-year average annual UK disbursements to above codes (2022–2024)
  • LOW estimate: £486m/year (3-year average)
  • HIGH estimate: £661m/year (3-year average)

The computation is reproducible via scripts/compute_child_health.py in the source repository.

Step 3 — Your contribution to child health (C)

HMRC's Annual Tax Summary allocates every taxpayer's bill across government spending areas using a single denominator: total expenditure on services (£1,156.4bn in 2024–25). We use the same denominator to convert your tax into a proportional share of UK child health ODA.

C = T × (childHealthODA ÷ expenditure on services)

C is the estimated amount of your tax that reaches child health programmes through UK official development assistance in a given year.

We also show a per-day figure: C ÷ 365.

  • Expenditure on services: £1,156.4bn — HMRC — Your taxes and public spending, 2024–25

Step 4 — What C buys (unit costs)

We convert C into three tangible items using published procurement prices. All costs are in USD and converted to GBP at the Bank of England 2024 annual average rate (1 USD = £0.7874, source: Bank of England).

  • Pentavalent vaccine dose (DTP-HepB-Hib): $0.75–$2.50 per dose — UNICEF Supply Division vaccine price data, 2024
  • Bednet (LLIN) distributed: $3.50–$6.00 per net including logistics — Against Malaria Foundation distribution data, 2023
  • Full ORS + zinc treatment (per WHO/UNICEF joint guidance for childhood diarrhoea): $0.14–$0.50 per treatment — UNICEF Supply Division — ORS and Zinc, 2024 (low = ORS only, 2 sachets; high = ORS + zinc co-pack)

Step 5 — Monte Carlo uncertainty

We are not certain whether the LOW or HIGH definition of child health ODA better reflects where UK money has the most impact — and unit costs vary by country and year. To show this uncertainty honestly, we run 10,000 simulations, each time drawing childHealthODA uniformly from between the LOW and HIGH values, and drawing each unit cost independently from a uniform distribution between its low and high values.

The results shown are the 10th–90th percentile range across those 10,000 draws, with the median (~50th percentile) shown as the typical estimate.

This means: in 80% of plausible scenarios, the true figure lies within the shown range.

What this estimate does not include

  • Multilateral aid via UNICEF, World Bank, and other multilaterals (beyond Gavi) — these are harder to attribute to specific health sub-sectors and are not included.
  • Non-monetary UK contributions (technical assistance, policy influence).
  • Tax paid outside Income Tax and NI (e.g. VAT, capital gains) — methodologically complex and beyond the HMRC Annual Tax Summary methodology we follow.
Disclaimer: This is an illustrative estimate, not an official breakdown of your tax. It is built in support of ONE's Born to Live petition and is not an official ONE product. Figures are based on publicly available government and international data; they should not be cited as an exact measure of government expenditure.
